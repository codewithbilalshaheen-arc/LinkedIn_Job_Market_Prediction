================================================================================
PROPOSED RESEARCH EXTENSION:
TEMPORAL JOB MARKET DEMAND & EMERGING SKILLS DYNAMICS FOR SOFTWARE STARTUPS (2023–2024)
================================================================================

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & CONTEXT OF THE ORIGINAL PAPER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The foundational paper ("A Deep Learning Framework for Predictive Feature Prioritization
in Early-Stage Software Startups: Integrating Historical Delivery Data and Market Signals",
MDPI Computers 2026, 15, 380) proposes a dual-branch deep learning architecture
designed to optimize feature prioritization for nascent software companies:
  a) Historical Feature Encoder: A two-layer Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory
     (BiLSTM) network with hidden size 128, dropout, and an attention mechanism
     trained on internal sprint delivery metrics (story points, cycle times, velocity,
     historical defect rates) across three private early-stage software startups.
  b) Market Signal Encoder: A one-dimensional Convolutional Neural Network (1D-CNN)
     leveraging pre-trained word embeddings and parallel kernels of sizes 3, 4, and 5
     (100 filters each, 300 total pooled units) followed by global max-pooling to
     extract external market signals from public software job postings.
  c) Multimodal Fusion & Decision Head: Concatenation of the sprint delivery representation
     and market signal representation into a 128-unit ReLU dense layer, outputting a
     binary prioritization probability via sigmoid activation trained with Binary
     Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss.

2. RESEARCH MOTIVATION & IDENTIFIED LIMITATIONS OF THE ORIGINAL STUDY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
While the dual-branch framework provides a compelling conceptual bridge between internal
engineering capacity and external commercial viability, critical methodological and
practical limitations remain:
  a) Static Market Representation: The original paper treats job market postings as a
     static, time-invariant corpus. In reality, modern technology sectors experience
     rapid, volatile shifts in skill demand, architectural patterns, and framework
     obsolescence (e.g., generative AI tooling, cloud infrastructure evolutions).
  b) Closed-Source Historical Data Bottleneck: The historical sprint records were
     derived from three private, anonymized software startups. Because this dataset
     is proprietary and unreleased, the full dual-branch architecture cannot be
     independently audited, benchmarked, or reproduced by the broader scientific community.
  c) Published Reporting Inconsistency: The published paper reports an untouched test
     set of N = 24,823 observations, yet the reported confusion matrix sums to
     exactly 9,930 observations (TN=4,821, FP=1,012, FN=734, TP=3,363). This 2.5x
     discrepancy underscores the necessity of open, verifiable evaluation protocols.
  d) Neglect of Skill-Level Trajectories: The original 1D-CNN encodes global semantic
     n-grams but does not explicitly quantify the temporal acceleration, velocity,
     or saturation of individual core competencies over time.

3. PROPOSED EXTENSION: TEMPORAL JOB MARKET DEMAND & EMERGING SKILLS ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To overcome these limitations without fabricating unverified private sprint records,
we introduce a rigorous, open-science research extension:
"Temporal Job Market Demand and Emerging Skills Dynamics (2023–2024)."

This extension augments the static market signal encoder by modeling the longitudinal
evolution of technical skill demand and experience requirements across temporal windows:
  - Temporal Posting Velocity: Quantifying day-by-day and week-by-week job posting
    volumes and identifying seasonal, macroeconomic, or platform hiring surges.
  - Emerging vs. Saturated Skill Identification: Computing relative demand velocity:
      Growth_Rate(s) = [Count_t2(s) - Count_t1(s)] / Count_t1(s) * 100%
    where t1 and t2 represent partitioned longitudinal observation windows.
  - Experience Level Trajectories: Tracking the ratio of entry-level (low market
    priority proxy) versus senior/lead roles (high market priority proxy) over time
    to measure startup hiring conservatism versus expansionary talent acquisition.
  - Domain and Compensation Dynamic Mapping: Correlating emerging technical skills
    with annualized salary distributions and remote-work flexibilities.

4. DATASET COLUMNS SUPPORTING THE PROPOSED EXTENSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
From the Kaggle LinkedIn Job Postings (2023–2024) corpus (`postings.csv`, N=123,849):
  1. `listed_time` / `original_listed_time`: Epoch timestamps (millisecond precision)
     converted into ISO-8601 temporal coordinates (Year-Month-Day, UTC).
  2. `title`: Job position titles used to filter engineering/startup roles and extract
     domain specializations.
  3. `description`: High-dimensional raw text parsed with regular expressions and NLP
     entity extractors for 30+ technical skills (Python, PyTorch, Docker, Kubernetes,
     AWS, React, GenAI, LLM, SQL, CI/CD, Rust, Go, TypeScript, etc.).
  4. `formatted_experience_level`: Canonical experience tiers (Entry level, Mid-Senior,
     Associate, Director, Executive, Internship) tracking organizational seniority shifts.
  5. `formatted_work_type`: Remote, Hybrid, and On-site designations analyzing flexibility
     trends in emerging engineering demands.
  6. `normalized_salary` / `min_salary` / `max_salary`: Real-valued compensation
     benchmarks evaluated against emerging skill premiums.

5. METHODOLOGY & EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATION FRAMEWORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Step 1: Temporal Validation & Epoch Normalization
    - Filter job postings with valid, non-corrupted epoch timestamps.
    - Segment records into standardized temporal windows (e.g., bi-weekly or monthly).
  Step 2: Lexicon & Skill Taxonomy Extraction
    - Establish a standardized dictionary of high-relevance technical skills spanning
      AI/ML, Cloud/DevOps, Backend, Frontend, and Data Engineering.
    - Extract frequency occurrences across tokenized job descriptions per temporal slice.
  Step 3: Growth Velocity and Acceleration Metrics
    - Compute baseline frequency, end-period frequency, absolute delta, and percentage
      growth rate for each technical competency.
    - Rank skills into 'High Growth / Emerging', 'Stable Core', and 'Declining / Saturated'.
  Step 4: Cross-Tabulation with Priority Proxies
    - Correlate emerging skills with the binary market priority target (y = 1 Entry level,
      y = 0 Experienced/Senior roles).
    - Determine whether early-stage startups prioritize emerging tools for junior roles
      or retain them for senior architectural leads.

6. EXPECTED RESEARCH CONTRIBUTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Methodological Transparency: Demonstrates how public market corpora can be rigorously
     mined for dynamic product and talent planning without relying on opaque private records.
  2. Empirical Skill Trajectories: Delivers reproducible quantitative evidence on emerging
     technical stacks (e.g., Generative AI, Kubernetes, TypeScript) within the 2023–2024
     hiring landscape.
  3. Feature Prioritization Heuristic: Provides startup founders with an evidence-based
     market radar: features requiring rapidly emerging competencies carry higher market
     interest but may encounter steeper talent acquisition hurdles.

7. LIMITATIONS AND ASSUMPTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  a) Platform Specificity: Data is sourced exclusively from LinkedIn; postings may over-
     represent larger tech enterprises and venture-backed startups compared to bootstrapped
     early-stage entities.
  b) Proxy Characterization: Experience level (`formatted_experience_level`) is an external
     commercial proxy for feature complexity and organizational priority, rather than a
     direct measurement of an internal JIRA sprint backlog item.
  c) Observation Window: The specific Kaggle snapshot spans dense clusters between March
     and April 2024; findings reflect active springtime hiring campaigns and should be
     interpreted within this operational window.
